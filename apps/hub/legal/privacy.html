Privacy policy

Last updated 2 October 2026 (anonymous usage counters added)

The short version

  • No accounts. No sign-up, no password, no email address. You never type your name anywhere.
  • Your progress stays in your browser. Chips, scores, settings and play time are saved on your own device and are not sent to us.
  • Three things are sent: Parking Precision’s own leaderboard scores, with a made-up name (section 5); anonymous usage counters, such as which game page was opened (section 6); and the club boards, which show a made-up name with minutes played, best Blackjack chips, Parking stars and longest streak (section 7). You can switch each off.
  • No ads, no cookies, no tracking across other sites, and no third-party analytics on Sundown Club.
  • Nothing is loaded from other companies. Fonts and code are served from this site itself.
  • Play money only. The chips in Blackjack, Hold’em and Video Poker are not real money and never can be.

1. Who runs this

Sundown Club is a small collection of free browser games made by Daksh Anajwala. It covers everything at sundown-club.vercel.app: the homepage, Blackjack, Texas Hold’em, Video Poker, Parking Precision and the Night Drive test drive. Questions go to daksh.anajwala@gmail.com.

Deleting it: “Save & settings” also has an erase button, or clear this site’s data in your browser’s settings and all of it is gone. Parking Precision also has Settings → Reset progress. Tutorials use pretend chips and save nothing while they run.

3. Hosting

The site is hosted by Vercel. Like every web host, Vercel’s servers see standard connection details in order to send you the pages, such as your IP address, browser type and the time of each request. We don’t look at, collect or use those logs ourselves. They are covered by Vercel’s privacy policy.

Analytics: Vercel Web Analytics is not switched on for Sundown Club, and no page here loads a third-party analytics or measurement script. The only counting is the club’s own, described in section 6.

Parking Precision used to live at its own address, parking-precision.vercel.app, which still works. That older site has Vercel Web Analytics switched on (counts of visits only, no cookies) and has its own privacy policy. Playing it here, inside Sundown Club, does not use that analytics.

4. Nothing from other companies

The fonts (Young Serif, Schibsted Grotesk, IBM Plex Mono, Big Shoulders Display) and the code libraries the games use (three.js, GSAP, cannon-es) are copied onto this site and served from it. Your browser never contacts Google Fonts or any other content network to show a page here. The only links to other sites (such as GitHub) are ordinary links that do nothing until you click them.

5. Parking Precision’s leaderboard

This is the only data that leaves your device, and it only applies to Parking Precision.

It is automatic, and you can turn it off

When you finish a Parking Precision level, your score for that run is posted to a public board. The first time, the game tells you and offers a one-click way to stop. You can also turn it off at any time in Settings → Leaderboard → “Post my scores automatically”. With it off, nothing is sent unless you press Post yourself.

What is sent

  • When a level starts: a request for a one-time “run token”, so a score can later be checked against the time our server measured. It contains only the level number.
  • When a score is posted: the level, your score, stars, time, how many things you bumped, the made-up name you were given (for example “Swift Otter 412”), a random identifier that keeps that name yours, and the moment it was posted.

The name is generated from a fixed word list, so nobody can put their real name, or anything else, on the board. The identifier is a random string made the first time you post. It is not a fingerprint: nothing about your device is measured to make it, and it cannot recognise you on any other website. Clearing this site’s data gives you a new one.

Where it goes

Requests to sundown-club.vercel.app/api/… are passed on to the Parking Precision server at parking-precision.vercel.app, which belongs to the same owner and keeps the board in a database run by Upstash. Posted rows are public: anyone can read the board. Only your best run per level is kept.

To stop one person flooding the board, the server briefly notes the IP address a request came from (it arrives with the forwarded request, as is normal on the web). That count expires by itself within ten minutes and is never stored with your score.

Removing your score

Email daksh.anajwala@gmail.com with the name shown on the board and the level, and the entry will be deleted. Because there are no accounts, that name is the only way to find it.

6. Anonymous usage counters

So the club can tell whether anyone comes back and which games get played, each page sends a few small messages to the club’s own server (sundown-club.vercel.app/api/club/event). It is on by default and you can turn it off at any time (see below).

What is sent

  • The random identifier from hub.v1.profile (made in your browser, never tied to your name, and never your homepage name).
  • Short event names such as “opened Hold’em”, “unlocked an achievement” (the achievement’s id, so the club can show what share of players have each one), “finished a round”, “left the page”, and a few tags: which game, how long the page was on screen, how many days since you first came, which test group you are in when the club is running an experiment (for example “round panel: off”), and a rough source for the visit (direct, from this site, search, social or other; the address you came from is not sent).
  • Nothing you typed, no name, no email, no chip counts or scores, and no details about your device.

What is kept

Counters, and per-day lists of random identifiers (to count how many different browsers came on a day and came back later). No single event is stored on its own, and the lists contain no name. Everything is deleted automatically after 180 days. The server briefly notes the IP address of a request to stop floods; that count is scrambled with the date, expires within two minutes and is never stored with the counters.

Turning it off

It stays off if your browser sends “Do Not Track” or Global Privacy Control. It can also be turned off with the “Anonymous usage counters” switch at the bottom of the homepage, which saves telemetry: false in hub.v1.settings. When off, nothing is queued or sent. Clearing this site’s data gives you a new random identifier, so the old one can never be linked to you again.

The usage numbers are read only by the owner, behind a password, as totals and percentages.

7. The club boards

The homepage has a section called “The board”: who has played the most minutes this week, the most Blackjack chips held, the most Parking stars and the longest run of days. It is on by default and has a switch (“Show me on the boards”) right there.

What is sent

  • Play time: when a game page opens it asks the club’s server for a session; then, only while the page is on screen and you have pressed a key or moved the pointer in the last two minutes, it sends one small “still here” message a minute. It names the game (for example blackjack) and nothing else. Hidden or idle pages send nothing, and the server counts at most 75 seconds per message, six hours a session and sixteen hours a day.
  • Blackjack and Parking numbers: your best chip count, your Parking star total with your best park, and your final stack in the Daily Blackjack tournament (the best of the week is shown). These come from your browser, so they can be edited by someone who tries; the board says “unverified” and the server refuses impossible values.
  • The random identifier from your profile goes with each message. No name you typed is ever sent. The name on the board is made up by the server from that identifier (for example “Amber Heron 42”). “New name” makes another, up to three a day.

What is kept and who sees it

The server keeps your made-up name, the minutes you played in each game (this week and in total), your best chips, stars and streak days, and the identifier those hang on. Anyone can read the top ten and see names with numbers and which games they play; the identifier is never shown. This week’s time board is deleted after 60 days, the others stay until you switch the boards off. The server notes your IP address for a few minutes to stop floods (scrambled with the date, never stored with your numbers).

Sharing: friends, invites, challenges, ghosts

These also use the club boards switch; with it off none of them sends anything.

  • Friends: “Make a club” creates a six-character code (letters and numbers only) that stands for a private board. People who type the same code see each other’s made-up names and minutes. The code is the only thing you ever type to the club’s server, and it cannot hold anything else.
  • Invite links: a short random code that stands for you (never your identifier). When someone opens the link and finishes a first round, the server records that this identifier arrived through that code, once, so each of you gets a token (five a month for the inviter).
  • Challenge links: when you tap Share on a Video Poker hand, the server signs a code containing the shuffle number, your result and your made-up name, so the numbers cannot be changed. Anyone with the link can see them. Nothing is kept on the server.
  • Parking ghosts: “Get a ghost link” uploads one run’s driving path (positions and heading, no name) with its level number and your made-up name. It is kept for 30 days under a random eight-character id and shown to anyone who opens the link. A friend’s ghost you race is saved in your browser as parking-precision:rival:v1.
  • An invite code you arrived with is kept in hub.v1.invite until you finish a first round.

Switching it off

The switch takes you off every board at once, frees your name and stops every message. Clearing this site’s data gives you a new identifier and leaves the old rows behind, so email daksh.anajwala@gmail.com with the name that was shown and it will be deleted. The identifier is stored in your browser as part of hub.v2.profile; the switch itself is saved as board: false in hub.v1.settings, and hub.v1.board remembers the last numbers sent so they are not sent twice.

8. Offline play, installing and reminders

  • Offline play: the club saves copies of its own pages, code and pictures on your device (in your browser’s cache, with a small helper called a service worker) so it opens quickly and works without a connection. Nothing is sent when it does this, and clearing this site’s data removes the copies.
  • Installing: if your browser offers to install the club like an app, we only ask politely after your second visit. “Not now” is remembered and we do not ask again. Installing is done by your browser.
  • Reminders (off unless you turn them on, and only if the club has them switched on): after your third visit we may ask whether you want one reminder a day. If you say yes, your browser gives the club a push address (a link at the push service run by your browser’s maker, such as Google, Mozilla or Apple) together with your random identifier. Once a day, at most, the club asks that service to wake your browser, which shows a short generic message that the Daily Seed is up. The message contains nothing about you. The push service sees that a message was delivered to your browser. Turning reminders off in “Save & settings” deletes the address from the club’s server.

These are saved on your device as hub.v1.pwa: how many times you have opened the club, and whether you said “Not now” to the install or reminder questions.

9. Play money

The chips in Blackjack, Hold’em and Video Poker are play chips. They cannot be bought, sold, cashed out or swapped for anything, and nothing you win has any value outside the game. No payment details are ever asked for.

10. Children

We don’t knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. There is no account and no place to type a message. The homepage lets you change the name it calls you, but that name stays in your browser and is never sent or shown to anyone else. The only public thing, the Parking Precision leaderboard, uses its own made-up names. If you think something identifying has ended up on the board, email us and it will be removed.

11. Changes

If the way Sundown Club handles data ever changes (for example analytics, ads or accounts), this page will be updated before the change goes live, and the date at the top will change. Where the law requires it, you will be asked before anything new happens.
