Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 28, 2026

This is short because the game does very little with data. Read it end to end — it's five sections.

1. There is no account, and the game itself runs on your device

Parking Precision runs entirely in your browser. There is no login and no password. Everything that makes the game work — physics, levels, scoring — is computed on your own device.

There is one exception: the leaderboard. When you finish a level, your score for that run is posted to a public board automatically. You can switch that off, and section 4 explains exactly what is sent, what it is stored against, and how to stop it. Nothing else about your play is sent anywhere.

2. What's stored on your device, and why

The game saves a few small things to your browser's localStorage: your progress (best score, stars, time and bumps per level, the best medal you have earned there and your fastest park time, plus your best park's offsets in each bay, for your "personal best" outline in the post-park review — stored under parking-precision:progress:v5; an older parking-precision:progress:v4 save is read once to carry your record over and otherwise left as it was); your daily challenge results (how many dailies you have parked in total, the number of the last one, and your best score, stars and time on today's — a plain count, nothing tracks consecutive days; parking-precision:daily:v1); your best run for the ghost car (when a run becomes your best on a level, the path your car took — position and heading, 10–20 times a second — so it can replay as a see-through car next time; at most about 30 KB per level and 400 KB in all, parking-precision:ghost:v1; switch it off in Settings → Personal best); your settings (graphics quality, field of view, driving position, volume, units, HUD layout, camera choice, personal-best display choices, rebound keys); and your leaderboard name and identifier (the randomly generated name you were given, e.g. "Swift Otter 412", and the random identifier it belongs to, so your runs keep posting under the same name — you did not type the name and neither value is derived from anything about you or your device).

That's the lot — no name you typed, no email, no location, and nothing measured about your device, browser or network.

  • Progress, daily results, ghost runs and settings never leave your device — they aren't sent to us or anyone else. Daily challenge runs are never posted to the leaderboard, and "Share result" only copies text (or opens your device's share sheet): the page itself sends nothing.
  • The leaderboard name and identifier do leave it, but only when a score is posted, and only to our own server (section 4). They are never shared further, and never used for advertising or tracking.
  • None of it is a cookie, technically, but it behaves the same way from your point of view: a small file your browser keeps for this site.
  • It is yours to delete — Settings → Reset progress clears your progress, medals, daily results and stored ghost runs; clearing this site's data in your browser removes everything above, the leaderboard name and identifier included.

We don't use it (or anything else) for advertising, tracking, or fingerprinting, and no third-party ad scripts are loaded by this game.

3. Hosting and analytics

This game is hosted on Vercel. Like any web host, Vercel's servers necessarily see standard connection information to deliver the page to you (IP address, browser type, request timestamps) in server logs. We don't access, collect, or use that ourselves — it's covered by Vercel's own privacy policy, not this one.

Vercel Web Analytics is switched on for this project at the hosting account. The game's own pages load no analytics or measurement script — you can check by viewing the page source. Vercel's own measurement covers aggregate traffic — page views, referring site, rough location (country/region), and device/browser type. It doesn't use cookies, doesn't fingerprint you, and doesn't track you across other sites: it's computed from anonymised, aggregated request data, not from anything the game itself sends. See Vercel Web Analytics' privacy policy for exactly what it collects. We use it only to understand how many people play and where the traffic comes from — never to identify an individual player.

If we later add ads or anything that sets cookies or tracks you across sites, this policy will be updated first, and — where the law requires it — you'll be asked for consent before it happens.

4. The leaderboard (automatic, and switchable)

The game has a public leaderboard, and it posts your score automatically when you finish a level. The first time that happens the game tells you so on the results screen and offers a one-click way to stop it. You can also turn it off at any time in Settings → Leaderboard. With it off, no score, name or identifier is sent unless you press Post yourself.

When a level starts, the game asks our server for a one-time run token, so that a score posted later can be checked against the time the server itself measured. That request contains only the level number: no name, no identifier, nothing about you or your device. It happens whether or not you end up posting, because the token has to exist from the start of the run.

Each posted run sends and stores on our server: the level you played; your score, stars, time, and how many bumps, cones and kerbs you hit on that run; the randomly generated name above; and the moment it was posted. That is the whole record. There is no name you typed, no email, no account, and no message field — the name comes from a fixed word list, so nothing personal can be put on the board even deliberately.

Your name, and the device identifier behind it. So that one player keeps one name instead of collecting rows under several, the server gives your browser a random identifier the first time a score is posted, remembers which generated name belongs to it, and refuses to post under any other name. The identifier is a random string. It is not a fingerprint: nothing about your device, browser, screen, network or behaviour is measured, collected or derived, and it cannot be used to recognise you on any other website. It identifies a browser, not a person — clearing this site's data or using a private window gives you a fresh identifier and a fresh name.

Posted rows are public. Only your best run per level is kept. Our server also briefly counts requests per IP address so one person cannot flood the board with runs or posts; that counter expires within ten minutes and is never stored next to your score or used to identify you. The board is hosted for us by Upstash and Vercel.

Removing your score: email the address below with the name shown on the board and the level, and the entry will be deleted. Because there is no account, that name is the only way to find a row.

Scores are worked out in your own browser, so they can be faked by someone determined enough. The board is labelled unverified in the game for that reason. It is for fun.

5. Children's privacy

We don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. There is no account, no free-text field, and no way to enter a name, an email address or a message — the leaderboard name is generated for you from a fixed word list precisely so nothing identifying can reach a public page, whoever is playing. If you believe something identifying has reached the board anyway, tell us and we'll remove it.
