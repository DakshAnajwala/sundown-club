Level 1 · Why this exists

I was supposed to be studying for a physics test.

Inclines. Friction. The forces on a car parked on a ramp.

I got bored and went looking for a parking game to play for five minutes.

None of them felt right.

So I closed the textbook and made my own.

— Daksh Anajwala

Level 3 · How to play

Stop inside the lines. Then shift to P.

Each level puts one bay in front of you, glowing green. Drive to it, park straight, come to a stop and select Park. You're scored out of 100.

Arrow keys steer and drive too; 1–4 pick gears in gate order. Every key here can be remapped in Settings.

If the driver's seat is too hard

  • Chase cameraSit behind the car instead of inside it. Press C any time.
  • Proximity radarA top-down view on the dash screen showing everything close to the car.
  • Parking sensorsBeeps that quicken as a bumper closes in, and a reversing camera in R.
  • Your own HUDTurn any panel off, resize it, remap every key, pick your dials.

Leaderboard

Every level has a board.

Finish a level and your score goes up automatically, under a name the game picks for you. No account, nothing to type, and you can switch it off in Settings. Scores are worked out in your browser, so treat the board as a bit of fun rather than a record.

Level 4 · How it's made

No models. No textures. All code.

Every car, wall and parking line in the game is built from code as simple shapes, and every sound is synthesised live in your browser. Nothing is downloaded but the game itself.

  • three.jsdraws the 3D world, the mirrors and the reversing-camera picture
  • cannon-essimulates the car: four raycast wheels, springs and grip, stepped 120 times a second
  • Web Audiomakes the engine note and the sensor beeps on the fly, no sound files
  • 4.20 × 1.78 mthe car's footprint, which every bay and aisle in the game is measured against
  • 3.56 mturning radius at full 42° lock, measured in the physics engine, not guessed
  • 10 raystwo five-ray fans, one on each bumper, feed the parking sensors
  • 137 carsparked across City Drive, merged into a handful of draw calls to hold 60 fps
  • 0 assetsno models, textures, sound files or fonts inside the game — only code

Before you start

Is it free?
Yes. No ads, no account, nothing to buy.
Do I need to download anything?
No. It runs in an up-to-date desktop browser with WebGL switched on.
Can I play on my phone?
Not yet. The car is driven with a keyboard, so it needs a computer.
I've never parked a car. Is it too hard?
Start with the tutorial: it walks you through gears, steering and reversing in a few minutes. If the driver's seat is too hard, press C for the chase camera.
What do you collect?
Your progress and settings stay in your browser. Only a finished run's score goes to the leaderboard, and you can switch that off. The privacy policy has the details.
